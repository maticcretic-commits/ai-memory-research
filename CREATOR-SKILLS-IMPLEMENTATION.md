## Method and limits

Researched from public channel descriptions, creator sites, course pages,
and tutorial syllabuses available by web search on 27 October 2026. No paid
course, private community, template pack, or full video catalogue was
accessed, and no video was watched end to end. Subscriber counts differ
across sources and dates; ranges are given where sources disagree, and no
count is treated as proof of quality. Income and growth claims made by or
about creators are reported as claims, not verified results. Skills below
are the public patterns each creator teaches, restated in Jarvis's own
words. Nothing here copies a creator's proprietary template, prompt pack, or
course text.

## Part A — Skill inventories

### 1. Jeff Su — productivity systems powered by AI

Who: former Google product marketing manager, nine years at Google, trained
over 6,000 Googlers in practical AI use; channel @JeffSu, roughly 1.2–1.9
million subscribers across sources of different dates; 6–12 minute polished
tutorials. Focus: AI inside existing workflows, not AI as a separate job.

Skills taught:

1. **CORE workflow — Capture, Organise, Review, Engage.** Capture every
   task or idea immediately; organise with minimal friction; review in
   scheduled sessions; engage by blocking dedicated time. Tool-agnostic;
   becomes automatic in about two weeks on his account.
2. **AI systems that compound.** A personal AI toolkit configured once and
   reused: a master prompt, custom instructions, and a style guide, so a
   rough draft comes back already sounding like the user.
3. **Prompts database.** A Notion database of prompts with properties for
   type, use case, output format, app, and example output; captured prompts
   triaged, pinned prompts kept at hand, quick-action entry kept to one tap.
4. **Conventions file / rulebook.** A written rulebook an AI assistant uses
   in every new chat: which database and template, what property options
   mean, how pages should look, naming conventions. A connector gives
   access; the rulebook teaches preferences. Tested in fresh chats with and
   without the file, the organised output was the consistent part.
5. **Decision framework for new tools.** A reusable test for any new AI
   tool, model, or feature, so the choice takes minutes: where it fits, what
   it replaces, what it must prove before adoption. Techniques are taught as
   transferable patterns across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, not as
   copy-paste prompts for one platform.
6. **Workspace workflows.** Google Workspace with Gemini (Gmail, Docs,
   Sheets, Calendar, Meet), Notion workflows and a Command Center, meeting
   transcripts turned into action items, emails drafted in the user's own
   voice, spreadsheet formulas written from natural language, calendar and
   task management, note-taking and meeting tools.
7. **Content craft.** One real workplace problem per video, step-by-step
   structure, on-screen captions, tight editing, templates and prompts
   linked for reuse. Standout example: "10 ChatGPT Life Hacks" built on
   corporate use cases rather than generic prompts.

Similar channels researched: Tiago Forte (second-brain / PARA organising),
AI Foundations (foundational how-tos), The AI Advantage / Igor Pogany
(practical AI workflows for professionals), Matt Wolfe (AI tool news and
comparisons), Matthew Berman (model and tool reviews with demos), How I AI
(real applications and experiments), Skill Leap AI (practical tool
tutorials). OutlierKit's AI Tools & Productivity cohort also names Recall,
Tiago Forte, Rick Mulready, and Matt Wolfe alongside Jeff Su.

## Part C — Governance: what stays closed, and what opens it

Round 7 synthesis (27 October 2026) stands. No settlement is locked or
provisionally locked. The following stay closed, whatever Phase 1 builds:

1. **No real production decision pairs.** Zero exist in the record.
   Prototypes run on synthetic or staged pairs only, labelled as such.
   Staged results never substitute for real pairs.
2. **No sending channel.** Talk-back stays as gated proposals. No Vapi
   call, no Telegram message, no email send, no OmniDimension call, no
   API key, no account creation — until a sending channel is separately
   approved, with its own gate record, and Nitesh's explicit tap for the
   account and terms.
3. **No calibration claim.** The calibration gate stays FAIL. ECE is a
   diagnostic on synthetic pairs only. No threshold, no confidence-keyed
   routing, no claim that the 14-pair probe or the 80-pair run proves
   anything about real pairs.
4. **No appointment or value claim.** ISA/IGR remain unappointed,
   certification void. V remains undeclared for V1 and V2. No owner is
   invented for any record in this file.
5. **Fail-closed throughout.** Silence, delay, a missing signature, or a
   missing baseline never counts as approval. Every prototype logs its
   inputs, its decision record, and its outcome, or it does not run.
6. **Provenance and no silent overwrites.** Every memory write carries a
   source and a supersession link. Forgetting is tested by the red-team
   rule, never assumed. Secrets are blocked by the write gate, never by
   a prompt policy alone.

What opens each closed item is the record Round 7 named: appointing
bodies and appointees with funding; a signed entry-eligibility
declaration with a declared value; a calibration-gate authority with a
pre-registered threshold; live baselines (1,000 sessions, 50 users for
telemetry); a separately approved sending channel. Until then, Phase 1
and Phase 2 below are the whole programme.
