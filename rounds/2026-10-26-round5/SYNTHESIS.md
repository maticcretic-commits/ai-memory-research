## The five locks, in one line each
- DeepSeek (staged-pilot generator): an 80-pair generator with a
  pair-by-pair redundancy gate R1–R5 and a 14-pair design probe
  with go/no-go criteria G1–G5; passing the probe vets legibility
  only and never licenses real pairs.
- Grok (triple-gate numbers): 240 attack cases in three equal
  strata of 80, human-block rate ≥ 98%, calibration coverage
  ≥ 90%, recorded by an Independent Gate Recorder (IGR) as a
  timestamped, hashed, write-once certificate.
- ChatGPT (S4 adjudicator): the previously unnamed owner is named
  by role as an Independent Safety Adjudicator (ISA) with binding
  authority over disputed S4 cases and a fail-closed deadlock rule:
  no signature means UNRESOLVED, NOT CLEARED.
- Perplexity (first V values): no numeric V is established; the
  auditable starting workflow is accounts-payable invoice
  exception handling, with formulas and declarant roles frozen
  before results and refusal recorded as NOT DECLARED / BLOCKED.
- Gemini (active labels versus passive telemetry): pivot straight
  to passive telemetry for real production pairs, bypassing the
  planned 50-participant active labelling phase, on the ground
  that the measurement distorts what it measures.

## Convergences — all five, in different lanes
1. **The package is now locked as commitments, not results.**
   Every Round 5 reply states its own limit in the same breath as
   its lock: Grok's numbers are design commitments; DeepSeek's
   generator has generated nothing; ChatGPT's adjudicator is
   proposed and awaiting appointment; Perplexity's values are
   formulas without local inputs; Gemini's pivot is argued, not
   demonstrated. A locked package is not a passed package.
2. **Fail-closed is now the default in every lane.** No ISA
   signature, no S4 clearance (ChatGPT). Triple gate red, no real
   pairs (Grok). Value undeclared, class blocked (Perplexity).
   Probe no-go, back to authoring, never scale to 80 (DeepSeek).
   Active labels invalid on the stated triggers, abandon them
   (Gemini). In each lane the burden is on the claimant to
   produce the record, and silence, delay, or a missing signature
   never counts as approval.
3. **Independence is enforced by separation, not promised.**
   Two new independent roles now guard two different records:
   the ISA owns S4 dispositions and the IGR owns gate
   certificates; both sit outside the development chain, both
   write records builders cannot overwrite, and both have a
   protected replacement process. DeepSeek's third-party
   relabelling of rater conditions and Perplexity's separation
   of preparation, approval, and evaluation follow the same
   pattern: the person who makes the claim never certifies it.
4. **Disputes are never averaged, smoothed, or silently resolved.**
   DeepSeek logs rater disagreements as separate records with
   raw agreement plus κ. ChatGPT keeps unresolved S4 cases
   visible in reporting and forbids retroactive conversion.
   Grok logs residual ≤ 2% false negatives for severity
   adjudication instead of absorbing them into a pass. Gemini
   treats telemetry rejections as equal negative signals and
   names that flattening as a cost, rather than hiding it.
   Perplexity records refusal in the declarant's own words.
5. **Everything material is pre-registered before results.**
   Grok's suite cases carry expected outcomes and rubrics before
   the system sees the input. DeepSeek's rubrics and predicted
   directions are hash-stamped before any model output.
   Perplexity's valuation register freezes currency, price date,
   boundaries, formulas, source records, ranges, error costs,
   permissions, exclusions, and expiry before results are viewed.
   ChatGPT's six-element evidence chain must be complete before
   an S4 disposition can be signed. Gemini's pivot triggers
   (15% task slowdown, κ < 0.70 after ten interactions) are
   stated before any cohort runs.

## The central conflict: Gemini's early unlock versus gate discipline
Gemini's sequencing consequence breaks the discipline the other
four lanes just locked. If Cohort B undo rates fall below 2%, Gemini
would provisionally unlock the triple gate for low-consequence real
pairs *before* V numbers are resolved. That contradicts Grok's rule
(real pairs only when all three gates are simultaneously green, and
postponing after they are met is also a violation) and Perplexity's
rule (a class without a declared value stays out of value-based
real-pair evaluation). It also sits uneasily with Gemini's own
Round 4 position, where the pivot to passive telemetry was the
consequence of labels *failing* the κ ≥ 0.75 / inversion ≤ 1%
test — Round 5 pivots before that test runs, on the argument that
the test itself distorts the behaviour it measures. Both positions
cannot govern at once: either the label study runs and its failure
triggers the pivot, or the pivot is taken on the observer-effect
argument and the study never runs. The next round must settle which
rule governs, and what evidence — if any — could reopen the other.

## Other tensions and gaps that remain unlocked
- **Two independent recorders, one appointment problem.** The ISA
  (ChatGPT) and the IGR (Grok) are distinct roles guarding
  distinct records, but neither names an individual, an appointing
  body, or a budget authority, and neither reply says whether
  the same person could hold both roles. DeepSeek's open question
  sharpens this: who owns relabelling and the audit trail, and is
  that person independent of the ISA? Until appointing bodies are
  named, both roles are titles, not controls.
- **The telemetry why-gap is now the package's largest known
  blind spot.** Gemini states it plainly: passive signals show a
  rejection but not why — wrong memory, superseded memory, or bad
  timing all look identical — and subjective calibration becomes
  inferential only. Its own fallback, a micro-interaction labelling
  phase to recalibrate talk-back timing when response-time variance
  is high, reintroduces the very labelling it pivots away from,
  at smaller scale. Whether that compromise is coherent, or just
  the pivot with an exception, is unsettled.
- **The calibration FAIL still has no closing number.** Grok
  locks calibration *coverage* (≥ 90% of classes mapped and
  probe-tested) but not a calibration *accuracy* threshold that
  closes the underconfident-agent FAIL from Round 1. DeepSeek's
  third open question asks exactly this and leaves it open, and
  asks whether it gates the 14-pair probe or only the 80-pair run.
  Coverage without an accuracy threshold lets a class be mapped
  and still wrong.
- **Value is method-locked, number-free.** Perplexity freezes the
  formulas, the register, and the declarant roles, but no
  individual is named, no local record is cited, and no time,
  recovery, or false-hold estimate exists. The package can now
  say *how* a value would be locked; it cannot say what any
  value is. The AP workflow is also a single example, not a
  demonstration that the method generalises to other classes.
- **Transfer from simulated to real remains unproven by design.**
  DeepSeek's generator, Grok's synthetic suite, and Gemini's
  staging A/B test all run off production. Every reply says so
  explicitly. The 80-pair pilot, the 240-case suite, and the
  passive-telemetry baseline can each vet a design; none can
  certify that simulated behaviour transfers to real decisions.

## What is locked, what is not — scorecard
Locked (as commitments): triple-gate numbers (240 / ≥ 98% /
≥ 90%, IGR certificate, stratum balance 25–40%, re-adjudication
κ ≥ 0.80); ISA role with fail-closed deadlock rule; pilot
generator with R1–R5 and G1–G5; V formulas and register shape;
passive-telemetry pivot triggers (15% slowdown, κ < 0.70).
Unlocked: ISA individual and appointing body; IGR individual and
reviewer count for a stable κ sample; S4 appeal mechanism and
operational evidence specification; V numbers, declarant names,
and local records; calibration accuracy threshold closing the
FAIL; telemetry why-gap resolution; simulated-to-real transfer.
Experiments run: still 0 of 5 designs.
