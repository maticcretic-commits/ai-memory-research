The appointing body alone may appoint the ISA, remove the ISA for cause (documented conflict, failure to apply the six-element evidence chain, or material breach of recusal), and secure the ISA’s operating budget. (c) S4 Appeal Mechanism — DRAFT

Appellant class. Any party that is the subject of an S4 disposition, or any party whose operational authority is directly constrained by that disposition, provided the party is not the development-chain entity that originated the disputed claim.

Grounds. (1) Incomplete six-element evidence chain; (2) material procedural irregularity (failure of recusal, undisclosed conflict, or use of an unappointed ISA); (3) clear factual error in the certified record that, if corrected, would change the disposition.

Filing window. Thirty calendar days from the date the ISA certificate is issued or from the date the appellant first receives notice of the disposition, whichever is later. Untimely filings are rejected without review.

Interim treatment. The disputed S4 disposition remains in force (fail-closed) pending final decision of the appeal. No automatic stay is granted; the appellant may request interim relief only from the deciding body below, which may grant it solely on a showing of irreparable harm that itself satisfies the six-element chain.

Deciding body. An ad-hoc Appeal Panel of three members drawn from a pre-approved external roster maintained jointly by the ISA and IGR appointing bodies. The panel may not include the ISA who issued the original certificate, any member of the development chain, or any person who holds or has held the IGR role. The panel’s decision is final and does not collapse the ISA/IGR separation: it reviews only the specific grounds alleged and either affirms, vacates, or remands to a newly appointed ISA.

Effect of successful appeal. The original certificate is vacated; the case returns to UNRESOLVED, NOT CLEARED status. A new ISA (appointed under the same charter rules) must re-adjudicate de novo. No automatic clearance is conferred by a successful appeal.
