Recommendation: declare local values, not a universal price

Memory Milestone should predeclare V separately for each decision class: a signed monetary ceiling per additional successful decision over retrieval-only. No public evidence verified here establishes an appropriate dollar ceiling for mm. The defensible first values are therefore locally calculated commitments—not borrowed benchmarks or retrospectively fitted prices.

The proposals below resolve the cost rule without introducing another readiness score. They do not verify mm’s internal claims, resolve its reported calibration failure, or authorize proactive sending.

First values by decision class

Because no operational decision taxonomy was supplied, use these four classes for the first declaration. Assign each eligible decision to one class before evaluation; do not pool their economics.

Routine, reversible workflow decisions — V1: documented marginal cost of the cheapest acceptable existing method for converting one comparable failure into success—for example, manual correction. If unavailable, use the refusal rule below. Who declares it: Workflow budget owner, with finance validating costs and the workflow owner defining success.

Recommendations with measurable financial outcomes — V2: conservative incremental contribution margin or avoidable cash loss attributable to one additional success, capped at the cost of an acceptable alternative remedy where available. Exclude gross revenue, speculative lifetime value, and benefits already counted elsewhere. Who declares it: Accountable business owner and finance; an evaluator checks attribution and outcome definitions.

Talk-back: surface, remind, warn, ask, explain, silence — V3: the applicable underlying workflow ceiling, minus expected downstream costs omitted from the experiment’s cost numerator. If talk-back has no defined downstream decision outcome, do not declare a positive value for “speaking successfully.” Who declares it: Workflow budget owner and the person accountable for affected users’ attention and consent.

High-consequence or externally consequential decisions — First operational ceiling: V4 = 0 additional expenditure until a domain-specific value and independent permission requirements are signed. This is a temporary governance restriction, not a finding that these decisions lack value. Who declares it: Domain-accountable executive, finance, and relevant safety, legal, privacy, or professional authority.

These formulas and the zero default are proposed policy choices. No verified public source establishes their numerical values for mm. They become usable thresholds only when owners substitute documented local inputs and sign actual amounts before results.

For labor inputs, use employer compensation rather than salary alone. BLS explicitly measures wages plus benefits per hour worked; that supports the costing method, not a claim that saved minutes become realizable savings. Owners must separately document whether time releases capacity, avoids hiring, or reduces expenditure.

How to declare and apply the ceiling

Use one signed declaration on the existing experiment scorecard. Record the class, accountable owner, currency and price date, success rubric, outcome horizon, retrieval-only comparator, cost boundary, numerical Vc, supporting records, and stop/pivot/scale rules.

Define:

ΔSc = Smm,c − Sretrieval,c, ΔCc = Cmm,c − Cretrieval,c.

Here, ΔSc is net additional successes over comparable opportunities—not merely mm wins with losses ignored. Report paired wins, paired losses, ties, and denominators. Include incremental inference, storage, review, correction, interruption, and operating costs; predeclare how fixed costs are amortized and report research expenditure separately.

For positive ΔSc, the economic condition is:

ΔCc / ΔSc ≤ Vc.

Also report incremental net benefit:

INBc = Vc ΔSc − ΔCc.

This avoids interpreting a misleading ratio when additional successes are zero or negative. The net-benefit construction has an established public analogue: incremental benefit multiplied by its value threshold, minus incremental cost. Its transfer to decision successes is a proposed application, not a validated mm measure.

Predeclare uncertainty handling too. My proposed scale rule requires positive incremental success and a positive lower uncertainty bound for incremental net benefit, alongside separately passing permission and safety gates. Specify the interval method and level beforehand. Microsoft’s experimentation guidance supports defining falsifiable hypotheses, success metrics, guardrails, and power before testing; it does not establish this particular scale rule.

For talk-back, count improved downstream decisions—not accepted proposals, message openings, or apparent confidence. Include eligible silence and abstention in the declared outcome rubric. Proposal-only evaluation cannot establish the value of an actual sending channel.

What public evidence establishes—and does not

NICE’s economic-evaluation manual provides a useful structural precedent: compare additional costs with additional outcomes against alternatives, and assess uncertainty. Its health-outcome thresholds are not transferable prices for AI decisions.

NIST likewise says risk tolerance is contextual, application-specific, and not prescribed by its framework. It calls for organizational accountability and warns that laboratory measurements may differ from operational risks. This supports assigning declaration authority to accountable owners rather than allowing mm to set its own willingness-to-pay ceiling.

Neither source supplies empirical mm values. Retrieval accuracy, green tests, or attack-test results would not independently establish those values even if verified.

If stakeholders will not predeclare

Record “value undeclared”; do not substitute a convenient dollar figure. Stop the value-confirming experiment and any cost-effectiveness-based scaling decision for that class. A separately approved, capped measurement exercise may investigate baseline costs or outcome definitions, but cannot establish economic readiness.

If a value is declared after results become visible, retain those results as exploratory and require a fresh prospective evaluation. Permission and safety restrictions remain independent of willingness to pay.

Top 5 findings

1. No verified public evidence supplies a defensible dollar-per-success value for mm.
2. Initial values should come from documented local alternatives or attributable economic benefits.
3. Count net decision improvement, including regressions—not isolated wins.
4. Talk-back inherits value from downstream decisions; speaking is not itself success.
5. Refusal to predeclare blocks economic scaling, not necessarily bounded measurement.

Top 3 open questions

1. Which concrete workflow supplies the first auditable success outcome and replacement-cost record?
2. Who controls its budget and can commit a numerical ceiling before seeing results?
3. Can evaluation capture downstream outcomes, attention costs, and mm-induced losses?

Sequencing consequence and limits

A signed, evidence-backed ceiling plus prospectively demonstrated incremental net benefit could move that class ahead of further retrieval-only optimization, subject to independent gates. Conversely, no measurable benefit or no willing declarant would keep baseline measurement first.

This piece deliberately does not prove mm’s reported figures, production effectiveness, safe thresholds, calibrated behavior, or permission to act or send. No experiment is claimed to have run.
