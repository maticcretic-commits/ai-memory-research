Cost-of-being-wrong adjudication: safety-first versus build-first sequencing for Memory Milestone

The unresolved conflict is sequencing priority. Safety-first insists on a synthetic ledger, red-team forgetting exercises, a controlled talk-back gate, reproduction with participants, and only then any real-pair pilot; any earlier real pairs constitute a process violation. Build-first prioritizes provenance plus secret blocking, read-time re-scoring, and an immediate 14-pair pilot while deferring the consolidation gate and the full red-team suite. Both orders accept the same foundational claims already established in prior rounds: SQLite as authoritative record with hybrid retrieval and metadata filtering first, versioned supersession that preserves history and forbids silent overwrites, talk-back as a distinct layer (surface, remind, warn, ask, explain, silence), per-claim and per-action confidence with high confidence granting no automatic permission, a shared one-page scorecard of raw counts and denominators without a merged readiness score, and pre-commitment discipline that fixes stop/pivot/scale and cost rules before results appear. The figures attached to the system (829 green tests, LongMemEval-S R@1 0.712 / MRR 0.797, six red-team rounds reaching zero critical success) remain internal claims only; they have not been independently verified and supply no license to treat any threshold as proven.

The explicit cost-of-being-wrong criterion is the expected harm of the dominant failure mode under each sequencing error, measured in irreversible leakage or irreversible opportunity cost, not in abstract preference for caution or speed. Unproven forgetting on real data is the more costly error. Real-pair data introduces persistent identifiers, secrets, and decision-relevant history that cannot be fully sanitized after the fact. If the forgetting machinery (supersession, retention rules, red-team deletion paths) has not been stress-tested to a measurable bound, a single successful extraction or residual reconstruction attack yields lasting exposure: secrets that were supposed to be gone remain recoverable, provenance chains that were supposed to be broken remain linkable, and any downstream decision that relied on the erased material becomes non-auditable. The damage is not confined to a laboratory score; it travels with the data. By contrast, unproven value on synthetic data is costly mainly in deferred learning and wasted cycles. Synthetic ledgers can be regenerated, re-seeded, or discarded. An agent that looks competent on synthetic pairs but later proves inert or harmful on real pairs wastes calendar time and erodes credibility, yet the primary asset—real user data—never left the controlled boundary. The asymmetry is structural: one error contaminates an irreplaceable resource; the other contaminates a disposable proxy.

This does not license indefinite delay. Safety-first must itself be challenged with equal force. Prolonged confinement to synthetic data can entrench optimistic calibration, hide distribution shift, and produce a false sense of readiness precisely because the agent never confronts the long-tail secrets and adversarial motives that real pairs contain. An agent that has never been allowed to fail on real data may accumulate high internal confidence on a non-representative distribution; when the pilot finally occurs, the first real leakage or the first worthless decision arrives after organizational commitment has already risen. Build-first therefore correctly pressures for early, tightly scoped real contact. The correct resolution is not a permanent ranking but a measurable flip condition.

The order should flip from safety-first to a controlled real-pair pilot when three jointly observable thresholds are met and recorded on the shared scorecard before any real data is admitted:

Forgetting red-team: zero critical residual reconstruction or secret recovery across a pre-specified number of independent attack suites on the synthetic ledger, with attack success defined by explicit recovery of a planted secret or decision-critical claim that the system was instructed to forget.
Talk-back gate: the surface/remind/warn/ask/explain/silence layer produces proposals only, never auto-sends, and the fraction of high-confidence proposals that a human reviewer would have blocked remains below a pre-committed rate.
Calibration gate: the under-confident agent path is no longer failing; the system’s expressed confidence intervals on synthetic decision pairs cover the observed error rate at the pre-committed coverage level.

Until those three are simultaneously green on the scorecard, real pairs remain prohibited. Once they are green, the 14-pair pilot may proceed under the same pre-committed stop rules that would have applied to any earlier experiment. This condition is deliberately stringent on the forgetting axis because that is the higher-cost failure mode, yet it is not infinitely stringent; it supplies an exit ramp rather than an open-ended veto.

Challenging the safety-first order itself reveals its principal vulnerability: it treats synthetic competence as a necessary but insufficient proxy while under-weighting the cost of never observing real failure modes early enough to correct them. A pure safety-first sequence can therefore produce the very over-confidence it claims to prevent—high internal metrics on a sanitized distribution followed by abrupt contact with real secrets. Build-first’s corresponding vulnerability is the opposite: it treats early real pairs as low-stakes probes when they are in fact the highest-stakes probes, because the data they introduce cannot be un-introduced. Both orders therefore require the same discipline: pre-committed numerical gates, raw denominators on every scorecard, and an explicit statement that high confidence never authorizes action.

The higher cost remains unproven forgetting on real data. The measurable flip condition is the simultaneous satisfaction of the three gates above. Any sequencing plan that admits real pairs before those gates, or that indefinitely postpones real pairs after those gates, violates the cost-of-being-wrong criterion.

Top 5 findings

1. Unproven forgetting on real data is costlier than unproven value on synthetic data because real secrets and identifiers cannot be fully recalled once exposed.
2. Safety-first’s principal risk is calibration drift and deferred discovery of distribution shift; build-first’s principal risk is irreversible leakage.
3. A joint, pre-committed triple gate (forgetting red-team zero critical, talk-back proposal-only with bounded human-block rate, calibration coverage) supplies the only defensible flip condition.
4. High internal confidence metrics remain non-authorizing; they never substitute for the gates.
5. Both orders are process violations if they ignore the pre-commitment rule that stop/pivot/scale criteria must be fixed before any new data class is introduced.

Top 3 open questions

1. What exact numerical values for the three gates (attack-suite size, human-block rate, coverage level) should be locked before any further synthetic work, and who records the lock?
2. How should residual reconstruction be operationally defined so that “zero critical” is inter-subjectively measurable rather than interpretive?
3. Once the flip condition is met, what is the minimal real-pair sample that still allows an early stop without contaminating the larger cohort?

A result that would change the sequencing plan is empirical demonstration, on the shared scorecard, that the three gates can be satisfied on synthetic data at the pre-committed thresholds while a parallel, fully isolated synthetic “shadow pilot” simultaneously shows decision value above a pre-committed floor; that combination would justify immediate transition to the 14-pair real pilot. This piece deliberately does not prove that any gate has been met, that any internal figure is accurate, that any threshold is safe, or that either pure order is free of residual risk; it only ranks the costs and states the observable condition under which the ranking reverses.
