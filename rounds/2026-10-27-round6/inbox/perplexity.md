1. Gemini's early unlock versus gate discipline

Status: unlocked; proposed early-unlock settlement unverified.

No proposed early unlock demonstrably survives the V rule on the supplied evidence. A class without a declared value stays blocked. An early unlock that bypasses that declaration contradicts the rule; a narrower proposal allowing another, already-declared class to proceed might be compatible, but no class-specific approval record is available.

The critical distinction is between collecting evidence and unlocking value-based evaluation or action. Permission to gather passive observations must not silently become permission to proceed through a gate.

Record required to move one step: the exact early-unlock clause; the affected decision class; its authorized, signed V declaration and valuation basis; the proposed activity's scope; and an independent gate review identifying which prerequisites have passed and which remain blocking. This could support provisional acceptance of a bounded pathway—not certify its eventual benefit.

2. Appointing bodies for ISA and IGR

Status: unlocked; appointment settlement unverified.

No proposed appointing body can be verified against the separation rule because neither the appointment instruments nor the relevant identities and reporting relationships are supplied. I do not expand ISA or IGR or assign them invented institutional owners.

Appointment and certification must be distinguished. A body may nominate a reviewer without establishing that the reviewer is independent of the claim-maker. Conversely, an “independent” title does not establish who controls the evidence, decision, or appeal.

Record required to move one step: each body's charter, appointment authority, named appointees, claim ownership, conflict disclosures, recusal rules, certification authority, and documented acceptance. The record must show that the person making the claim cannot certify that claim. These are audit requirements, not newly appointed bodies.

Falsifier: a claim-maker signing their own certification, a purportedly independent reviewer acting under the claimant's certification authority, or an undisclosed role overlap that defeats the stated separation.

3. Telemetry why-gap compromise

Status: unlocked; compromise unverified.

The precise compromise is unavailable. Passive traces may document what happened without establishing why a person acted. Adding a “quick” reason prompt changes the arrangement into active elicitation; brevity alone does not demonstrate that it avoids the four failure modes you attribute to Gemini: dual-task penalty, label fatigue, over-trust, and latency.

Thus, a micro-interaction proposal could reintroduce those risks. The record establishes neither that it does so nor that it avoids them. Separately, public discussion of automation bias supports scrutinizing over-reliance rather than treating human participation as automatic protection.

Record required to move one step: the exact prompt, timing, frequency, skip behavior, exposure to system advice, and separation of observed events from inferred motives. Supply a prospective comparison against passive-only collection, with declared measures for primary-task performance, repeated-use label quality, inappropriate reliance, and end-to-end delay. Acceptance criteria must precede the results; none are established here.

Falsifier: unacceptable task interference under those criteria, deterioration with repeated prompting, labels tracking the system's suggestion rather than independent reasons, or delay exceeding the declared allowance. Failure to identify motives would also falsify a claim that the why-gap was closed.

4. Calibration closing threshold

Status: unlocked; closing threshold unverified.

No calibration closing threshold survives as a demonstrated real-world threshold on the evidence described. Taking your premise that all current evidence is staged or synthetic, a threshold may be a candidate for testing—not a verified closure condition.

The problem is transfer, not merely the size of the staged score. Published calibration research explicitly distinguishes controlled synthetic perturbations from naturalistic distribution shifts and cautions against treating the former as realistic out-of-distribution evaluation. That supports the limitation, not any particular threshold for this system.

Record required to move one step: the exact candidate threshold; calibration target, decision classes and uncertainty method; a frozen evaluation protocol; independently adjudicated real decision–outcome pairs; and a prospective comparison of staged and real performance. A fully specified protocol could provisionally establish how closure will be judged, while leaving threshold validity unlocked.

Falsifier: failure on real pairs under predeclared criteria, material subgroup or temporal failures hidden by aggregation, or threshold selection after inspecting the supposed validation set.
