# Study added to AI memory research — interactive visualisers (2026-10-27)

Added at Nitesh's order on 2026-10-27: three user-supplied sites, verified live the same day from their own pages (text fetch only; no simulation was run, no account created, no pricing or licence checked beyond what each page itself states). Filed as study material for the AI memory programme, under BRIEF.md's "everything else" scope and the "how memory talks back" question: these are three different ways a system can SHOW rather than TELL — run a simulation, visualise a circuit, animate how a thing works.

No experiment has been run on any of them. No partner round has been launched on this study. Nothing here has been integrated into the mm code in `source/code/`.

## 1. Chemistry lab experiment — NOBOOK Virtual Laboratory

- Link: https://chemistry-en.nobook.com/
- What it is, from its own page: "NOBOOK Virtual Laboratory" — a catalogue of virtual chemistry experiments.
- Examples listed on the page (verified 2026-10-27): preparing oxygen gas by heating potassium permanganate and the properties of oxygen gas; iron wire combustion in oxygen gas; water composition; dust explosion; measuring oxygen content in air; laboratory preparation and properties of carbon dioxide; investigation of calcium hydroxide deterioration; sublimation of iodine; reaction of sodium peroxide with carbon dioxide; laboratory preparation of ammonia; flame of an alcohol burner; charcoal combustion in air and oxygen gas; bombard gold foil experiment; checking airtightness of apparatus; laboratory preparation of hydrogen gas by displacement of water; activity order of metals.
- Relevance to mm: a simulation as a memory surface — a remembered procedure (reagents, apparatus, steps, expected observation) rendered as a runnable experiment rather than recalled as text. Raises the talk-back question in visual form: when should a memory system re-run/show a procedure instead of describing it, and what counts as a correct outcome?
- Open questions: does the lab record a run (inputs, observations, outcome) in a form that could be stored as a decision pair? What is the licence/pricing for classroom or reuse? Not checked.

## 2. Circuit visualisation / simulation — Tinkered

- Link: https://www.tinkered.ai/
- What it is, from its own page: "Build, Simulate & Deploy Hardware with AI | Tinkered" — "Describe what you want to build. Tinkered designs the circuit, writes the firmware, validates the system, and simulates the complete device in 3D before you assemble it." It presents itself as an AI hardware design platform: circuit, schematic, component research and bill of materials, board-ready firmware, 3D simulation, and deployment files, kept connected as the design is revised.
- Boards named on the page (verified 2026-10-27): Arduino (Uno, Nano, Mega, UNO R4 WiFi, Nano ESP32, Nano Matter), ESP32 / ESP8266, Raspberry Pi Pico / Pico 2, RP2040, STM32 / STM32 Nucleo, Teensy, BBC micro:bit, Nordic nRF52, Feather boards; the page claims 1,300+ supported development boards.
- Simulation claim, from its own page: "Tinkered runs the firmware with the circuit in a 3D electronics simulation, so you can inspect behavior, test interactions, and catch problems before assembling the physical prototype."
- Relevance to mm: a design memory that stays connected — prompt, circuit, firmware, schematic, and simulation as one record, with revisions kept aligned. That is the closest analogue in this study to mm's provenance + supersession problem: when one part changes, which dependent records must change with it, and what is the audit trail?
- Open questions: pricing/account terms beyond the page's "Price: 0 USD" additional-details line were not verified; simulation fidelity and whether runs are exportable/logged were not tested. Not checked.

## 3. How things work visualiser — Animagraffs

- Link: https://animagraffs.com/
- What it is, from its own page: "Animagraffs - Animated infographics about everything."
- Examples listed on the page (verified 2026-10-27): How a Fire Engine Works; How a NASCAR Cup Series Race Car Works; How a P-51 Mustang Works; How a Formula 1 Race Car Works; How City Water Purification Works; How a Sewing Machine Works; How Crypto-currency Works; How an Electric Guitar Works; How speakers make sound; Disc vs. Drum Brake; Supercharger vs. Turbo; How a Car Engine Works; How a Flat Screen Display works; Inside a Jet Engine; How the Human Eye Works; How Hard Disk Drives Work; How Self Driving Cars Work; How a Mechanical Watch Works; How Wankel Rotary Engines Work; How Electric Cars Work; Solar Cell & Module; ISS: International Space Station; How Airbags Work; How Credit Scores Work.
- Relevance to mm: explanation as animation — a complex mechanism decomposed into labelled, moving parts. For talk-back, the question is the same in miniature: a short, visual, inspectable answer instead of a paragraph, with each part labelled so the user can check it. It also shows the cost side: every visualiser is authored, not generated from a memory record, so who authors the visual, and what happens when the underlying facts change?
- Open questions: licence/reuse terms for the infographics and the YouTube versions linked from the page were not checked.
